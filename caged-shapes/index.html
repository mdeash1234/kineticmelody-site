What is CAGED?

CAGED is a way of seeing the whole guitar neck through five chords you probably already know: C, A, G, E and D. Each of those open chords can be slid up the neck and held with a barre, which turns it into a chord you can play anywhere. So the same chord, say C major, can be played in five different places, and each place uses a different one of the five shapes.

How to use this page

Pick a root and a chord type. The neck shows every note of all five shapes, each in its own color. Tap one of the five cards underneath to light up just that shape. The chord diagrams read the way a normal chord chart does: a dot is a finger, an open circle is an open string, and a × means do not play that string. The labels on the neck are the note names. A dot with a dark outline is the root.

Why is it worth learning?

A chord chart shows you how to hold one chord. CAGED shows you where that chord is everywhere on the neck. If your hand is already at the 8th fret, you can see there is a C right there. It helps you play the same chord higher up when you want a different sound, move between chords with less jumping around, and find where the notes of a chord are when you solo.

Common questions

Why is it called CAGED? The five shapes follow each other up the neck in the order C, A, G, E, D, and then the pattern repeats an octave higher. Where you start depends on the chord.

Do I have to learn all five at once? No. Most people start with the E shape and the A shape, the two classic barre chords, and add the others over time.

What about minor and 7th chords? Switch the chord type above. The minor and 7th versions are the same five shapes with one note changed. In minor the C and G shapes drop a string, because the full version is an awkward stretch that guitarists rarely play.

Why do some shapes start at the nut and some far up the neck? A shape has to put its root note on the chord you picked, so where it lands depends on the root. Some shapes land close to the nut for one root and high up the neck for another.
